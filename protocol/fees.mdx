The fee model is short enough to fit in three sentences. Launching costs a **flat creation fee in USDC**. The launchpad takes **no fee on trades**. The only trading cost is the pool's own **Uniswap v3 swap fee — 0.3% or 1%, chosen by the creator at launch** — which accrues to the permanently locked liquidity position and is split between the token's creator and the protocol.

Everything on Arc is denominated in USDC, gas included, so every number below is a dollar number.

## Launching

| Item | Cost |
| --- | --- |
| Creation fee | Flat, in USDC (read live from `feeConfig()`; shown on the create page) |
| Liquidity you must provide | 0 (the locked supply is the liquidity) |
| Network gas | about $0.01 on Arc |

The optional dev buy is not a fee: it's a normal first purchase at the public opening price, and the pool fee applies to it like any other swap.

## Trading

Every swap in the pool pays the tier the creator picked at launch: **0.3%** (cheaper to trade) or **1%** (more revenue per trade). It is fixed for the life of the token and applies on every platform, not just veto.fun. That fee doesn't go to a market maker or to us off the top: it accrues to the pool's liquidity position, and the entire position belongs to the locked vault.

There are no deposit, withdrawal, account, or subscription fees of any kind, and no extra fee added by the Veto app or its swap router.

## Where the fee goes

The locked position's accumulated fees can be collected at any time (by the token's fee recipient, the treasury, or the platform's claimer service). Each harvest is split at the ratio snapshotted into the token at launch:

| Recipient | Share |
| --- | --- |
| Token creator (or the wallet they route it to) | 50% |
| Protocol treasury | 50% |

The split is immutable per token: whatever terms a token launched with, its pool pays out at those terms forever. This is the protocol's entire revenue model. If tokens don't trade, nobody earns. The protocol's half can be routed in part to a separate fee wallet (used for $VETO buybacks); that only ever moves the protocol's own share.
